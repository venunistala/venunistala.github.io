## What was failing

Release validation depended on someone remembering to run the suites, and
regression results were updated by hand — so the status everyone saw lagged
reality, and the lag itself was invisible. Reusable work stayed in
individuals' heads rather than being published where other QA engineers could
find and use it.

## What I built

Test suites and Jenkins pipelines integrated with Docker environments, so
product releases were validated continuously against an environment that
matched what shipped. Jira Xray was connected with Katalon Studio for live
reporting, so regression results updated themselves and the manual updates
disappeared. Reusable utilities and integrations were published for adoption
across the internal QA community. And for test automation against the
database, I resolved reading through encrypted blobs, so encrypted data could
be tested and validated like anything else.

## What it now blocks

A release can no longer go out with "the suites were never run" as a hidden
possibility — continuous validation runs them by default. Stale regression
status is gone by construction: Xray shows what the suites reported, not
what someone typed. And database-level validation is not blocked by
encryption, because the encrypted blobs can now be read.

## What it still doesn't catch

Docker environments match production closely, not exactly — configuration,
data, and neighbouring services in production can all behave in ways the
containers never show. Live reporting shows what the suites checked; untested
paths have no results to report, and a green dashboard can quietly mean
narrow coverage. And published utilities are only as good as their adoption —
a team that rolls its own helpers keeps its own risks, whether the shared
ones exist or not.
